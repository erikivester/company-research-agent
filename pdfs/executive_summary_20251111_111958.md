# Archer Daniels Midland: Navigating Market Shifts with a Focus on Food Waste Reduction

ADM faces a challenging market landscape, but its scale and existing partnerships provide a unique opportunity to address food waste and drive both environmental and economic value. While recent financial reports indicate revenue declines, ADM's commitment to sustainability, as evidenced by its ESG score and participation in initiatives like the World Food Program USA and the U.S. Food Waste Pact, positions it to leverage food waste reduction as a strategic advantage.

## The Numbers Tell the Story: Revenue Declines and the Opportunity in Efficiency

ADM's revenue has seen a downward trend, with a 4.86% year-over-year decline in the quarter ending June 30, 2025, and an 8.95% decline in annual revenue for 2024, reaching $85.53B. This context underscores the importance of identifying new revenue streams and optimizing existing operations. Reducing food waste offers a tangible pathway to improve margins, minimize disposal costs, and potentially unlock new product development opportunities. The recent drop in crushing profit by 93% to $13 million, attributed to "muted demand due to the deferral of U.S. biofuel policy and challenges with international trade flows," highlights the vulnerability of certain segments and the need for diversification and resilience, which food waste reduction can support.

## Sustainability as a Strategic Imperative: Building on Existing Foundations

ADM's commitment to sustainability is reflected in its S&P Global ESG Score of **40** within the Food Products industry (as of August 22, 2025). While this score provides a benchmark, there's significant room for improvement, particularly in areas related to operational efficiency and waste management. ADM's 2024 Annual Report emphasizes its commitment to "developing innovative, sustainable solutions in agriculture, food and nutrition, industrial and consumer products, energy, and footprint." This broad commitment provides a framework for integrating food waste reduction strategies across its diverse operations.

## Partnership Ecosystem: Leveraging Existing Alliances for Greater Impact

ADM's partnership with the World Food Program USA since 2005 demonstrates a long-standing commitment to addressing global food security. Building on this foundation, ADM's participation in the U.S. Food Waste Pact, led by ReFED and World Wildlife Fund, signals a willingness to collaborate on national food waste reduction efforts. This participation provides a platform for ADM to share best practices, access cutting-edge research, and benchmark its performance against industry peers. Actively engaging with the U.S. Food Waste Pact can accelerate ADM's progress towards its sustainability goals and enhance its reputation as a leader in responsible food production.

## Supply Chain Opportunities: Where Food is Wasted and Value is Lost

Given ADM's extensive network of over **270 manufacturing facilities** processing grain and oilseeds, the potential for food waste exists at multiple stages of the supply chain. A detailed assessment of waste streams at these facilities, from raw material handling to processing and distribution, is crucial to identifying key intervention points. This assessment should consider factors such as spoilage, overproduction, quality control rejects, and packaging inefficiencies. By quantifying the volume and composition of food waste at each stage, ADM can prioritize areas for improvement and develop targeted solutions.

## The Business Case: ROI on Food Waste Reduction

Reducing food waste is not only an environmental imperative but also a sound business strategy. By minimizing waste, ADM can lower disposal costs, improve resource efficiency, and potentially generate new revenue streams through the valorization of byproducts. For example, waste streams could be converted into animal feed, compost, or energy through anaerobic digestion. Furthermore, reducing food waste can enhance ADM's brand reputation and attract environmentally conscious consumers and investors. A comprehensive cost-benefit analysis of food waste reduction initiatives can demonstrate the tangible ROI and justify investments in new technologies and processes.

## Next Steps: A Roadmap for Collaboration

To accelerate its food waste reduction efforts, ADM should consider the following steps:

*   **Conduct a comprehensive food waste assessment:** Partner with ReFED or other experts to conduct a detailed assessment of food waste across its operations, identifying key sources and quantifying the associated costs.
*   **Set ambitious reduction targets:** Establish clear, measurable, and time-bound targets for food waste reduction, aligned with industry best practices and the goals of the U.S. Food Waste Pact.
*   **Invest in innovative technologies:** Explore and implement technologies to prevent, recover, and recycle food waste, such as improved inventory management systems, advanced processing techniques, and byproduct valorization solutions.
*   **Engage employees and suppliers:** Foster a culture of waste reduction by educating employees about the importance of food waste prevention and incentivizing them to identify and implement solutions. Collaborate with suppliers to reduce waste throughout the supply chain.
*   **Publicly report progress:** Transparently track and report progress towards food waste reduction targets, demonstrating accountability and building trust with stakeholders.
*   **Explore byproduct valorization:** Investigate opportunities to convert food waste into valuable products, such as animal feed, compost, or biofuels, creating new revenue streams and reducing environmental impact.
*   **Benchmark against peers:** Actively participate in the U.S. Food Waste Pact and other industry initiatives to benchmark performance against peers and learn from best practices.

By taking these steps, ADM can transform its approach to food waste, creating a more sustainable and resilient business model that benefits both the environment and the bottom line.
